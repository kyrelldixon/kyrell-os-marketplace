# Extraction Questions

Question bank for extracting tacit knowledge. Use one question at a time. Wait for answer before next.

## Starting Questions

**Always start with a recent instance, not a description:**

- "Walk me through the last time you did this. What triggered it?"
- "Think of the most recent [X] you handled. What came in?"
- "Can you show me an example from this week?"

## Probing for Artifacts

**When they describe an action, ask what they looked at:**

- "What did you look at first?"
- "Where did you find that information?"
- "Can you show me what that looked like?"
- "What tool did you use to check that?"

## Probing for Decisions

**When they say "I decided" or "I checked":**

- "What made you decide [X] instead of [Y]?"
- "What would have changed your decision?"
- "What if [variable] had been different?"
- "Show me one where you decided the opposite"

## Probing for Hidden Logic

**When they use vague language:**

| They say | Ask |
|----------|-----|
| "I check if they're a good fit" | "Show me one you rejected - what made it a bad fit?" |
| "I usually..." | "When do you do something different?" |
| "It depends" | "What does it depend on? Give me an example of each" |
| "I just know" | "Walk me through one where you weren't sure" |
| "Sometimes I..." | "What triggers the 'sometimes'?" |

## Probing for Variations

**After understanding the main path:**

- "What's the weirdest case you've handled?"
- "When does this process break down?"
- "What makes you handle something differently?"
- "Are there exceptions? Show me one."

## Probing for Quality

**How they know they did it right:**

- "How do you know you got it right?"
- "What would a mistake look like?"
- "Has this ever gone wrong? What happened?"
- "What do you check before considering it done?"

## Probing for Boundaries

**What stays manual:**

- "Which part of this do you never want automated?"
- "What requires your judgment specifically?"
- "What part would be risky to hand off?"

## Red Flags - You Need More

- They're describing what they *should* do, not what they *did*
- No specific examples, just generalizations
- "I don't know, I just do it" (probe deeper)
- Clean linear flow with no variations (reality is messier)
- No failure cases mentioned
